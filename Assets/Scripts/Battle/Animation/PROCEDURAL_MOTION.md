# Infantry motion authoring

`EmpireBattle.AnimationAuthoring.ProceduralInfantryRig` captures a musket-holding base pose once and samples deterministic bone poses without an Animator, per-frame hierarchy discovery or root movement. Constructor discovery supports the 28 named Bannerlord infantry bones, retaining imported local rotations, translations and scales. Instantiate the assembled rig, sample `musket_idle` at time zero in the Editor, then construct the authoring rig.

```csharp
var motion = new ProceduralInfantryRig(assembledSoldier);
motion.Sample(InfantryMotionKind.March, normalizedCycleTime);
if (!motion.ValidatePose(out string issue)) throw new System.Exception(issue);
```

The original source faces **-Z**, with anatomical right **-X** and up **+Y**. That is the constructor default; `authoredFacesNegativeZ: false` supports a rig already normalized to +Z. The existing crowd bake can rotate final mesh vertices 180 degrees around Y. Anatomical rotation axes are converted through each bone's parent orientation. Small offsets are specified in world meters and converted with the pelvis parent's inverse transform, respecting the imported 0.0254 scale and inch-valued child translations.

March/Walk, Run and Charge loop at normalized time 0–1. Their opposing legs use a root-relative planted stance and lifted swing foot path, solved through the captured thigh/calf segment lengths with a forward-facing knee pole. Stance feet stay at their captured root-relative ground height while sliding backward to represent travel; swing feet lift about 0.06–0.13m and step forward. The hips have modest bob and roll, the torso has a small gait lean, and the musket-holding arms remain at their captured base pose. Root motion stays zero, so the stride is authored in the limbs and should be matched to formation travel speed during playback. This is flat-ground foot placement: terrain adaptation and root-motion synchronization remain external responsibilities. Charge uses a modest extra forward lean; final muzzle alignment still needs a visual check against the assembled weapon. An aim-pose baseline can be captured on a separate authoring instance if needed. Hit/Recoil are bounded reactions that return to the captured pose at time one. Death collapses and rolls the body near the root ground plane, retaining the final pose after time one. `ResetPose()` restores the captured base pose; repeated sampling cannot accumulate offsets.

These are procedural prototype motions, not motion capture or historically verified drill. They have no terrain adaptation, rigid hand-to-musket constraints, cloth physics, ragdolls or terrain-aware death alignment. Visual acceptance and frame-by-frame baking belong to the asset pipeline. `Bones`, `BoneCount`, `HasRequiredBones` and `ValidatePose` support that pipeline; validate every sampled kind over a full cycle, compare loop endpoints, and check that the root transform remains unchanged. Bounds checks detect non-finite values, unexpected scale changes and bones outside a configurable three-meter radius; they do not establish visual quality by themselves.

## Expanded locomotion and casualties

`Sample(kind, phase, variant)` now accepts three persistent gait personalities, varying stride, foot lift, sway, and lean. Walk, March, Jog, Run and Charge each have 12 baked frames per variant and faction/LOD. Jog/Run use a shorter stance than walking. `ClosedGripAuthoring.ApplyLocomotion` authors left/right butt-in-palm upright carries with a free swinging arm, a tilted upright carry, and two-handed diagonal carries. Weapon and palm contacts are solved offline. Soldiers retain the same variant across LODs; actual speed selects walking/jogging within the movement order. Existing stride-driven gait phase remains continuous.

Ten death variants cover different fall directions and asymmetric limbs. The casualty renderer chooses a stable variant and duration per soldier, retaining the final corpse. All variants release the musket/ramrod; selected variants also release the hat after the first two frames. These rigid pieces follow baked ballistic/tumbling paths and remain on the ground; they are part of the existing instanced death mesh, not additional physics objects. Existing nearby cannon ragdolls still take precedence and keep their existing attached equipment. The new gear falls use a flat local ground plane, not terrain collision.

The infantry inspection app exposes Walk/March/Jog/Run/Charge/Death, variant cycling, pause, timeline scrubbing and replay. Command-line screenshots support `--motion March --variant 0 --pose-phase 0.25` (zero-based variants; Death has 0–9). Rebuild with `InfantryAssetBuild.BakeCrowd`, verify all gait/death samples with `InfantryMotionVerification.Verify`, then rebuild the infantry battle.

## Posture transitions and action timing

Standing/kneeling changes now use shared GPU morphs with eased 0.32s lowering and 0.38s rising, tracked per soldier on the presentation clock. Interrupted reloads have a standing/kneeling bridge for every reload frame, and a completed reload has separate idle and aimed rise targets. The firing scheduler gives a previously kneeling soldier a rise window before the shot. `InfantryPostureBuild` generates these small transition assets from the existing poses; the inspector's Lower/Rise buttons scrub them directly.

Reload movement now takes approximately five seconds and then holds the completed pose for the rest of the simulation cooldown, rather than stretching the action across the default 15-second cooldown. The run-selection threshold is 1.65m/s, reachable at the normal 2.04m/s running speed. Gait phases still follow actual traveled distance, including pauses and reverse movement. Inspector jog/run playback is faster than marching instead of assigning every gait a one-second loop.

## Close-order presentation refinement

Line/column files default to 0.62m between centres; rectangular rank depth is at least 0.90m. Formation orders and custom frontage preserve these dimensions, including footprint bounds. Skirmish spacing remains separate. Existing distance-driven walking handles soldiers filling gaps and forming ranks.

Each faction/LOD now bakes three twelve-frame idle and incoming-fire sequences. Idle selection and full-cycle phase are stable per soldier, with restrained breathing, weight transfer, head turns and anchored feet. The 6.4-second idle loop includes a brief two-foot shuffle in the grounded-musket variant, then a longer settled pause. Shoulder rest tilts the barrel backwards about 17 degrees in source rig space; a grounded-musket variant provides an alternative. March/walk use a two-handed upright carry with a slight backward slope. The arm authoring checks still enforce torso clearance and hand contact. These are reference-informed procedural poses, rather than a certified reconstruction of a particular army's drill.

Musket volleys and artillery damage timestamp incoming fire. Stationary soldiers briefly brace with staggered response delays; scheduled aiming, firing, recoil, kneeling and reload continue to take priority. Reactions expire after 2.4 seconds per soldier and use the presentation clock, so pausing does not animate them.

A shared baked command hierarchy accompanies each battalion. A default 250-man line has one mounted colonel, four captains with sabres and visible revolvers, eight simulated pike-bearing sergeants, four colour bearers and two drummers. The colonel rides the existing procedural horse rig with seated legs, animated walking/idle poses, saddlecloth, gold edging, sash, plume, bridle, reins and stirrups. Captains occupy living soldier slots near the front of their platoons; sergeants replace eight musketmen and spread across the front rank (or outer square perimeter); colours and drummers follow behind them. Columns put command figures along the flanks, squares contain them inside the bayonet perimeter, and skirmish formations use a single compact party. Counts reduce with surviving strength and the party disappears below eight men.

Regimental colours measure 1.02 × 0.75 m (about 25–28% larger in each dimension), with a taller staff and a separate forked pennant. Subdivided, double-sided cloth bakes broad travelling billows, smaller ripples and free-edge droop, while the hoist stays pinned. The British diagonals use exact polygon boundaries. Both factions and both LODs share the existing atlas, including unchanged uniform UVs. Staggered phases prevent the whole regiment waving in unison. All command figures remain GPU instanced, without runtime skeletons or cloth simulation, and are culled beyond 250 m. The colonel, musicians and colours remain decorative. Captains are four persistent combatants in a full regiment, with normal wounds/casualties, 1.4 m sabre reach and 40 base slash damage. Their revolvers fire only within 18 m, hold six rounds plus twelve reserve rounds, cycle every 1.4 s and reload in 12 s. They avoid friendly firing lanes and do not carry muskets. Sergeants have persistent soldier identities, normal wounds and casualties, no firearm, 3 m target reach (3.15 m strike tolerance), and 51 base thrust damage versus 34 for a bayonet. Dead sergeants are not automatically replaced. Small formations receive fewer sergeants (one per 32 men, rounded up, capped at eight); light infantry and cavalry do not receive pike sergeants. Colours remain generic faction designs.

It checks front placement across formations, casualties without promotion, ranged exclusions, and reach/damage at three simulation timesteps. Pike ready, thrust, knockdown, get-up and death poses are interpolated and GPU instanced; the inspector exposes all five.

`RegimentCombatVerification.BakeAndVerify` verifies both active command roles after baking. Captain ready, slash, revolver fire, knockdown, recovery and death poses share the crowd shader; close sidearm shots use the actual captain/target positions, firing effects and casualty accounting.
