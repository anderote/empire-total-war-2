# Black-powder battle effects

## Individual rifle aiming (development, 2026-10-02)

Volley, platoon and independent fire now choose a living soldier inside the targeted regiment before scheduling the shot. `InfantryTargeting` prefers the shooter's lane and nearer ranks, checks range/arc/terrain, and skips casualty gaps. `FireTargetSoldiers` and `FireAimPoints` snapshot that selection; accurate bullets use the same body point, and inaccurate bullets disperse around it. Targets are chosen again for the next shot; bullets already in flight remain ballistic and resolve actual contacts.

The soldier's facing, muzzle/lock offsets and smoke direction share that aim. A lateral muzzle offset is included in the aiming angle, and controlled fire allows time for each soldier to turn. Both managed and job-based movement retain individual facing during aim/recoil, then return to formation facing. `InfantryTargetingVerification.Verify` checks all three fire modes, square/skirmish, empty formation centres, dead-target replacement, muzzle alignment, both movement paths and the existing drill/bullet-contact regressions. This is a source change; it does not refresh the saved playtest.

`CombatSystem` captures the firing soldiers before simultaneous casualties are resolved. `RifleFireTiming` spreads their presentation times over 0–0.5 seconds, with a small number of late shots out to 0.85 seconds. Smoke, flash, recoil/recovery, sampled bullets and sampled individual gunshot audio share these timestamps. The two living physical firing ranks remain the simulation's shooting policy; formation slots are compacted after casualties, and volleys wait while men reform. Muzzle and lock anchors are calibrated from the imported rifle and central interpolated firing pose when building the infantry app.

`MusketSmoke` emits a dominant 2–3-second muzzle jet and a smaller 1.2-second lock puff for every captured rifle. Two reserved slots per represented soldier protect fresh gun smoke from eviction by cannon effects or old clouds. Future-dated puffs remain scheduled until their shot time. The separate 16,384-slot cloud pool merges the frontage into 32–42-second powder clouds and 44–49-second haze. Repeated volleys obscure the rendered enemy ranks, with integrated common gusts and seeded eddies opening gaps. Shader curl, translucent filaments, porous edges and sunlight scattering keep the cloud from appearing like a glowing solid sheet. `ActivePuffs` counts live puffs; `VisiblePuffs` counts the frustum-culled draw set. Billboards sort back to front and draw in batches of 256.

`MusketFlashView` draws brief yellow-orange muzzle cores, smaller lock flashes, ballistic muzzle sparks and occasional lock sparks. The eight closest flashes can use short-range, shadowless shared lights. Flash exposure responds to the directional sunlight. The meshes and sparks are instanced; there are no particle or light GameObjects per soldier.

`BattleProjectileView` presents sampled, non-luminous bullets at their rifle's muzzle/time. Ground misses kick up short-lived dirt and grit. A spatial grid of bounds proxies for instanced rural buildings, fences, trees and hedges provides masonry/wood chips and fluttering foliage debris along sampled curved paths. This avoids physics colliders per tree. Confirmed soldier hits keep their existing blood/casualty presentation. Small-arms dust lasts roughly 1.5–2.5 seconds; it does not accumulate like cannon powder.

Runtime effects use the interpolated simulation clock and respect pause and speed controls. After battle resolution the aftermath clock lets final scheduled shots and smoke finish. Camera movement still updates billboards, transparency order and culling while paused. Audio has a bounded scheduler, sampled sharp cracks, a rolling volley body, low echoes, distance filtering and sound-travel delay (see `AUDIO_STATUS.md`).

These effects are artistic approximations. Formation damage is still resolved per volley rather than at each presented projectile's arrival. Scenery impact proxies do not implement destructible buildings, exact mesh collisions or gameplay cover. Ordinary powder smoke obscures the rendered scene but does not change AI knowledge or accuracy; lime has a separate exposure-driven accuracy penalty. Clouds can be evicted under extreme sustained fire; protected rifle jets cannot be evicted by that cloud pool. Transparent billboard intersections may still show ordering artifacts. Soft depth intersections remain optional. No external audio recordings are used.

Validation: `BattleEffectsVerification.Verify` checks the existing simulation, protected smoke at 40,000 simultaneous rifles, finite deterministic audio layers, early/late shots, matching projectile/muzzle timestamps and nearest scenery impacts. `BattleEffectsVerification.BuildMac` runs the checks and builds the playable infantry app. Native player benchmarks and screenshots validate rendering separately.


Artillery now uses shared excavated height sampling and refined local ground/collider meshes, rooted crater smoke, angular ballistic soil/wood debris, procedural muzzle and incendiary flames, and ember/flash lighting. Tree trunks split into retained stumps and falling crowns; later impacts scatter existing corpses. See `ARTILLERY_STATUS.md` for controls, bounds and remaining approximations.

## Slow-motion emphasis

Playback below 1× boosts nearby blood, smoke and shell presentation, strongest at 0.1× and progressively lighter at 0.25×/0.5×. At full emphasis, blood sprays use 2.2× droplets with 40% larger rendered drops and 30% larger newly created body pools. Existing smoke is 25% wider and 45% denser. Detonation cores are 65% larger, soil bursts use 75% more clods, and explosions gain up to 24 sparks plus stronger brief light. The effect has full strength within 150 m and fades away by 450 m.

Normal and fast playback retain their original emission counts. All effects keep simulation-time playback and existing fixed pools; extra blood droplets do not create extra permanent stain entries. The boost never changes casualties, damage, explosion radii, terrain scars or ammunition. Smoke and droplet size respond to the current speed; particles already emitted finish their existing lifetimes when resuming, and deposited body pools remain. `SlowMotionEffectsVerification.Verify` checks real emission counts, pool saturation, distance falloff and unchanged combat state.

Slow motion: blood, thrown corpse fallbacks and detached limbs advance each rendered frame using scaled playback time. Ragdolls use stable 120 Hz physics steps with skeleton interpolation every frame; this keeps the solver independent of playback speed. Cannonballs and canister use ballistic presentation positions over the remaining combat tick, with terrain clearance and trails reset at skips. Rifle projectiles already use the continuous presentation clock. Explosion spray uses smaller, shorter droplets and a tighter fan; every landed spray droplet, including melee blood, creates a permanent terrain-conforming wet splash that dries with simulation time. Body pools remain anchored to settled casualties.

Shell blast damage and knockback use a 5 m radius (formerly 10 m); carcass shells use 4 m (formerly 7 m). Corpse scattering uses the same radius. The former emissive sphere is replaced by a torn, 0.28-second flash and seven short flame jets, seven uneven dirt jets, a low dust skirt and separate powder billows. Ground bursts use 47 pooled puffs; airborne/contact bursts use nine powder puffs without ground dirt. Flying soil uses 80 clods before presentation multipliers and peaks at 15 m/s. Clod rotations are normalized during integration to prevent invalid render matrices. Craters emit sustained smoke for 90 simulation seconds, holding full strength for the first 35 seconds before fading.

Carcass shells leave fire for 90 simulation seconds (formerly 18), with an 18 m burn radius (formerly 10). The visual filling now splashes forward along the incoming trajectory: 18 airborne globs arc into seven irregular fuel pools, with localized embers, animated fire sprites and buoyant smoke. The main splash reaches roughly 9 m forward; ground flame cards are roughly 1.3–2.15 m high. Fuel, flames, smoke and splash landing points share one seeded footprint, and hazards retain their impact direction after projectile reuse. This smaller visual footprint does not change the existing radial damage area. Carcass impacts throw 24 soil clods before presentation multipliers. Their crater radius is 55% and depth 35% of a comparable explosive shell; initial blast damage and its 4 m radius are unchanged. Fire still damages either army every three seconds with radial falloff.

`ArtilleryEffectsVisualVerification.Verify` checks footprint rotation/bounds, retained trajectory, ground versus airborne emissions, unchanged combat state and particle saturation. `Capture` runs offscreen editor renders across simulation frames (including a rotated elevated view) using the saved effect profile; invoke through `Tools/unity_editor.py --graphics --self-exit`. It rejects render errors and writes the review images to `Artifacts/ArtilleryEffectsReview`. It does not launch or publish a game.

## Carcass fire animation and attached burning effects

Carcass ground fire now uses the official Unity VFX sample `SmallFlame01_15x4` flipbook, interpolating its 60 frames with independent phase, speed and mirroring per card. The old 28-by-24-segment ribbon mesh is used only for muzzle presentation; ground fires use two quads per fuel patch within 80 m, one beyond. A nearby seven-patch fire therefore uses 28 flame triangles instead of 1,344. All ground and attached fire cards share one instanced material and batches of 256; 4,096 is the hard card limit. Frustum culling, distance limits, compressed mipmapped textures and no extra per-unit lights constrain rendering cost. These counts are geometry/submission budgets, not measured full-battle FPS improvements.

`EmitFireSmoke` feeds the existing bounded, depth-sorted smoke pool from the actual fuel patches and burning soldiers. The `WispySmoke03b_8x8` flipbook replaces per-pixel procedural noise for this smoke type. Smoke starts around flame height, rises continuously, expands and increasingly follows the common wind. Ground smoke emits three puffs every 0.2 seconds nearby, reduced to two every 0.9 seconds beyond 90 m and stopped beyond 225 m. Fire smoke has its own 4,096-puff cap within the shared pool, with expired or replaced puffs returning that budget. Previous smoke remains in world space when a source moves.

Carcass blast contact and existing fire-damage pulses notify the view through `SoldierIgnited`. Up to 128 nearby soldiers carry attached fire; standing, knocked-down and physical corpse positions are handled separately. Each exposure refreshes a five-second visual burn-down. This adds no damage, spread, panic or collision simulation. Existing hazard damage, duration and radial reach remain the gameplay authority. `CarcassFireVerification` checks actual exposure, moving smoke origins, expiry, unchanged damage and a 128-unit stress case: one ground fire plus those units produces 398 cards in two flame draws. Smoke uses separate sorted draws. The painted ember/fuel-bed overlay has been removed; only animated flame cards, sparks, smoke and actual terrain scars remain.

Research and provenance:

- [Unity texture sheet animation](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/PartSysTexSheetAnimModule.html): animated texture sheets and varied playback phases. Frame interpolation is implemented in the shared shader.
- [Unity particle-system comparison](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/ChoosingYourParticleSystem.html): VFX Graph supports GPU simulation at very high particle counts; ordinary particle systems provide direct C# control. This project already has a pooled renderer, so the implementation reuses that path without a new VFX Graph package or per-soldier emitter objects. This choice is project-specific, not a claim that VFX Graph is generally slower.
- [Unity graphics optimization](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/OptimizingGraphicsPerformance.html): transparent overlap, shader work, batching, culling, mipmaps and compression all matter. Pixel overdraw and complete battle GPU time still need native profiling before claiming an FPS improvement.
- [Unity official VFX samples](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/VisualEffectGraph-Samples): the two original fire/smoke textures are imported with source commit and license recorded in `Assets/ThirdParty/UnityVFX`. No sample project, simulation package or third-party paid asset is bundled.

## Dramatic artillery and ammunition revision (2026-10-02)

Shells now combine a brighter 0.28-second flash, seven fanned flipbook flame jets lasting 0.32–0.62 seconds, narrower dirt spikes with 4.8–10 m displacement, and higher upward clod velocities. These use the same fixed buffers and smoke count. Crater smoke reuses the existing 512-mark cache and 90-second fade, now with buoyant textured puffs from the crater floor within 225 m; it does not add a second crater emitter system.

All five ammunition types have a deterministic 7.5% chance to skip on a terrain strike with at least 25 m/s speed and incidence below 0.85. Each projectile can skip once, loses energy and starts a fresh trail. Shrapnel and steep/spent impacts cannot skip. Shell, carcass and lime fillings remain intact on the skip and discharge at their next collision. Roundshot no longer automatically makes four skips.

Lime has an expanding 24-puff chalk burst and eight distributed low cloud puffs every 0.8 seconds, covering its existing 16 m exposure radius for 60 seconds. An immediate exposure pulse replaces the previous three-second initial delay. Subsequent pulses retain their existing disruption amounts. Lime permanently reduces aim by up to 60% for this battle, scaled by the exposed share and proximity. Leaving the cloud does not remove that injury. Gun crews retain their service interruption. Neither side is immune. Lime also wounds infantry and gun crews for up to 6 health on the immediate pulse and every three seconds afterward, with linear falloff to zero at 16 m. This is one quarter of carcass fire damage per pulse. A healthy soldier remaining at the center dies after about 48 seconds; half-radius exposure across the full cloud leaves 37 health. Leaving the cloud stops further wounds immediately. Lime deaths use a distinct cause, with no explosion impulse or ignition; crews can be killed without destroying their carriage. Casualties receive normal artillery credit and morale pressure.

`ArtilleryImpactRevisionVerification` checks 10,000 deterministic rolls for every ammunition type, real skipped/ordinary terrain impacts, energy loss, no premature filling/shrapnel, single-skip termination, delayed discharge, and immediate/proportional/persistent lime precision loss. It also checks lime damage over four timestep sizes, friendly fire, falloff, leaving the cloud, expiry, casualty cause, absence of blast knockdown and crew kill credit. The offscreen capture now includes lime and the real crater-mark smoke path.

## Poison gas ammunition (development, 2026-10-02)

Poison gas is a sixth ammunition type (`PoisonGas`), appended so existing enum values and saved review presets retain their meaning. It is selectable as POISON GAS in battle and in the workbench, and unlocks at Artillery munitions rank 5 for regimental deployments. Lime remains a separate round.

The fictional gameplay cloud lasts 45 seconds across a 20 m radius. It delivers up to 6 health damage immediately and every two seconds with linear radial falloff. A healthy soldier at the center dies after 32 seconds, versus roughly 48 seconds for lime. Poison gas also leaves a movement penalty of up to 40% for the remainder of this battle, scaled by exposure; its temporary cloud obscuration still clears after leaving. Exposed troops from either army and artillery crews take damage, suffer impaired aim/service and award normal enemy casualty credit. Leaving the area stops damage; targets more than 2.4 m above the local terrain are clear. Gas does not throw bodies, ignite units, destroy carriages, excavate craters, fracture trees or release shrapnel. Ongoing hazard injuries prevent field-medicine recovery. Its 7.5% single ricochet follows the same whole-round policy as other ammunition; the filling releases only at the final impact.

Muted yellow procedural fog rolls over the local terrain with centers around 0.65 m above ground, flattened softly filled billboards and limited wind drift. A 28-puff initial release is followed by ten puffs every 0.8 seconds, fading during the last six seconds. The shared instanced, depth-sorted smoke renderer caps gas at 2,048 particles and stops cloud emission beyond 225 m. No individual particle objects, lights or new texture assets are created. These are game balance and presentation values, not a physical chemical-agent model.

`PoisonGasVerification` checks rank-5 progression, damage and falloff across four timestep sizes, prolonged core exposure, low-altitude exposure, lifetime/expiry, friendly fire, escaping survivors, crew casualties/credit, no explosive impulse/shrapnel, yellow tint and the gas particle budget. `ArtilleryImpactRevisionVerification` now covers all six ammunition types and ricochets; the visual capture includes poison-gas frames alongside shell, carcass and lime.

## Persistent cloud injuries and reduced damage

Cloud shells primarily impair survivors. Poison gas retains the worst exposed share as a battle-only movement injury (up to 40% slower); lime does the same for accuracy (up to 60% lower). Equal repeat exposure does not stack, partial exposure is proportional, and temporary obscuration cannot double the permanent accuracy penalty. Troops and artillery crews on either side are affected. These fields are excluded from serialization and reset when a new battle is created; regiment progression and equipment remain unchanged.

Movement reductions cover ordinary marching, running, cavalry, loose combat/retreat movement, formation corrections in scalar and Burst paths, crew walking and carriage hauling. Selected troop details show the lasting speed/aim reductions. Both clouds now deal only six damage at their centers per pulse: lime every three seconds for 60 seconds, poison gas every two seconds for 45 seconds. Damage stops on exit; injuries remain until battle end.

`PersistentCloudInjuryVerification` exercises proportional and capped injuries, repeat exposure, time independence after leaving, new-battle reset, friendly/enemy exposure, actual marching, scalar/Burst parity, cavalry speed, gun precision and hauling. It also runs the ammunition/lime regressions and poison gas gameplay checks.
