# Casualties and blood

`BattleWorld` retains one `SoldierDeath` record per dead soldier, including the original position, facing, cause and cannon impulse. Its death roster includes soldiers from destroyed battalions. Records last for the battle and reset on restart.

Deployment holds both the battle and casualty presentation clocks. Previously, the casualty clock advanced while `World.Time` remained zero, so newly killed soldiers could skip their whole fall after time spent deploying. `BattleRuntime.AdvanceSimulation` now gates both clocks together; the asset workbench retains its separate review stepping. `DeploymentDeathVerification.Verify` exercises the actual frame loop, a fatal Ming musket hit at both LODs, all four playback speeds, pause/resume and a persistent casualty without launching a player or campaign.

Artillery now has separate carriage health and four simulated crew members per gun. Solid shot and canister use the same ordered sweep as infantry hits; shells and carcass rounds burst on crew/carriage contact and apply local blast damage. Carcass hazards burn crews and carriages on either side. Lime clouds interrupt loading, aiming and firing without dealing explosive damage. Crew losses slow servicing; an uncrewed or destroyed gun cannot fire. Crew collision positions and displayed service stations share the simulation state. Dead crew play a fall and remain on the field; destroyed carriages retain a collapsed wreck. The battery UI shows operational guns and living crew, and Regimental Glory records lost guns for reports, saved strength and later replenishment.

A ground order on an enemy cannon tracks its battery. Each firing gun chooses an individual operational enemy cannon, distributes coverage by its own battery slot, and rotates targets on successive shots. Disabled and out-of-range pieces are skipped; automatic targeting also considers enemy batteries. Empty-ground orders keep their exact point. `CounterBatteryVerification.Verify` checks all five ammunition types, locality, timestep behavior, suppression/recovery, target distribution, crew animation and saved losses without launching a player.

Selected artillery now moves on an ordinary right-click on open ground, preserving the selected guns' spacing. Right-clicking an enemy attacks; **Bombard Ground** or **Alt-right-click** issues an explicit ground-fire order. Crews take two seconds to prepare movement, haul the carriage at a base 1.4 m/s (reduced by casualties, fatigue and terrain), and take three seconds to set up on arrival. Guns cannot fire or advance their reload during relocation; existing reload progress and fire/hold preference survive. Halt cancels movement, and attack orders stop the gun before setup. Surviving crews can haul guns out of lime. Deployment movement places guns immediately within their deployment zone. Routes use bounded obstacle detours; a route that cannot be cleared is rejected, and a newly blocked route stops the gun.

Regimental Glory replenishment buys as many missing artillery pieces as available coins allow, including a partially restored battery. XP, skills and issued kit are retained. Replacements deploy with working guns and full crews; replenishment remains a between-battle service. `ArtilleryMovementVerification.Verify` checks movement, setup, interrupted reloads, halt, crew loss, obstacles, deployment placement, move/attack/bombard commands, replacement costs and subsequent deployment, then reruns counter-battery and recruitment checks offline.

`BattleCasualtyView` plays the existing baked death poses for muskets and melee, keeping the final pose permanently. Roundshot and shell deaths within 350 metres of the camera use up to 96 physical skeletons in a separate, manually stepped Unity physics scene (32 are prewarmed; ordinary mounted musket casualties remain limited to 32). Cannon force launches and tumbles the torso. Terrain colliders follow the landscape height grid. Once grounded and settled, a physical body becomes a permanent mesh and its simulation slot is reusable. Distant cannon deaths and simultaneous overflow use baked deaths with permanent corpses. Ragdolls collide with terrain, not each other or living units; these effects do not alter combat or navigation.

## Cannon and canister update (development, 2026-10-02)

Infantry cannon ragdolls now include light infantry, regional outfits, sword troops, captains and pike sergeants. `InfantryRagdollAppearance` transfers common-rig skin weights onto their existing idle crowd meshes, preserving geometry and atlas; this is an approximate skin for outfits without authored ragdoll assets. Appearance skins are cached during battle setup. Long, slender connected weapon pieces stay rigid on one hand and receive ground-contact capsules. Common line troops retain their authored skins and dismemberment. Each frozen corpse retains its own material, including when a physics slot changes outfit. Infantry launches include a bounded share of movement momentum. Horses use a quicker heavy-impact collapse, short bounded skid and impact-dependent yaw; riders retain their physical separation.

Canister fires 96 actual swept projectiles per gun (previously 12), with a stratified horizontal fan and a shallow vertical spread. Projectile storage accommodates a simultaneous full volley from every gun. Pellets deliver 140 damage through the first 90 metres, tapering to 15% energy at 220 metres, and stop at the first contact. Mounted targets use a wider, taller horse-and-rider hit cylinder. Per-tick bounds are built from actual living soldier positions to skip unrelated units without missing stragglers.

`CannonImpactVerification.Verify` checks four-gun volleys at 60/110/210 metres and three timesteps, mounted flank/high hits, existing roundshot and ammunition regressions, physical deaths for eight outfit/command combinations, grounded poses, persistent corpses, pool reuse/overflow, shell fragmentation and horse impact direction. It captures offscreen infantry physics and horse-fall sequences under `Artifacts/CannonImpactReview`; it does not build or publish a player. Results for 120 exposed troops at 60 metres were 66 infantry or 49 cavalry deaths per battery volley. These development changes have not refreshed the saved playtest.

`BattleBlood` emits ballistic droplets and permanent ground spatter. A corpse's pool begins after its baked fall or physical settling, spreads over approximately 40 seconds, and transitions from wet red to dark dried blood over three simulation minutes. Pools conform to the terrain through a height texture. Overlapping footprints visually join. This is a surface approximation of liquid spreading, not a fluid solver; pools do not run downhill or fill terrain depressions. Ground marks are culled beyond 220 meters, retain their age when out of view, and return when the camera comes back. Droplets use a bounded 4,096-slot pool; ground marks and corpses persist until restart. Pause and speed controls apply to casualty physics and blood; these effects continue settling after combat ends.

Regenerate normalized infantry assets with `InfantryRagdollBuild.Bake`. `CasualtyVerification.Build` regenerates assets, checks death accounting and actual artillery provenance, runs the existing battle checks, and builds the infantry playtest. The exported mesh preserves the original skin weights, combines faction materials into an atlas and normalizes bone scales for physics.

For a repeatable in-player burst, pass `--casualty-inspect 1 --benchmark-seconds 12`. It creates 12 musket and 40 cannon deaths, verifies 52 persistent corpses, 32 frozen physical bodies, and permanent blood marks. Add `--screenshot /absolute/path.png --screenshot-delay 10` for a close view, or `--casualty-age 180` for dried pools. Use an absolute benchmark output and log path to preserve evidence. The inspection scenario does not run normal combat.

Validation: the in-player burst retained 52 corpses and 364 ground blood marks after all 32 physical bodies settled. CPU burst handling was about 6 ms and total corpse mesh snapshots about 7 ms on the development M4 Max. A roughly 2.3-second startup frame precedes casualties; steady-state p95 frame time was about 17 ms in the small inspection scene. These measurements are not a full-battle performance guarantee.

Expanded baked casualties now select among ten stable per-soldier death sequences at both LODs, with varied fall directions, limb poses and playback durations. Released hats, muskets and ramrods tumble and settle as part of the baked mesh, so they add no runtime rigidbodies or separate draw calls. Corpse endpoints persist, with offline ground-contact correction. This applies to baked musket/melee/distant/overflow casualties; the existing nearby cannon ragdolls still use their own physical motion and attached equipment. Gear uses a local flat ground plane; it does not collide with slopes or other soldiers.

Musket hits on infantry using the ten-variant library favor forward falls: 64% across quick pitches, staggered falls and buckled collapses; 28% backward and 8% sideways. `InfantryDeathMotion` gives the three forward falls distinct duration ranges (0.94–1.12, 1.18–1.42 and 1.40–1.68 seconds), including their existing rifle-release motion, so simultaneous casualties break the ranks at different speeds. Choices stay fixed per soldier and consistent across LODs. Command-role deaths, libraries without the ten authored variants, other causes and asset review retain their existing selection/timing. `InfantryDeathVerification.VerifyAndCapture` checks the mix and production meshes and renders an offscreen rank sequence without launching a game.

## Dramatic artillery and forward recovery

Blood hits now emit a directional jet and a wide fan of stretched ballistic droplets: 28 per musket/melee death and 72 per artillery death, with larger irregular splash marks at their landing points. The 4,096-droplet budget, terrain intersection, permanent pools, drying and simulation-time pause/speed behavior remain in place.

`ArtilleryGore` partitions the normalized physical infantry mesh by skeleton weights. Some lethal, energetic nearby artillery hits remove an arm, leg or head from the corpse and launch that actual uniform/skin mesh separately, with dark red wound caps. Removed geometry stays removed when the corpse freezes. Detached pieces tumble, bounce, settle above the terrain and remain until restart. Reused physical slots restore the complete original mesh before the next casualty. Dismemberment uses the existing 32-body close physics budget; distant casualties and simultaneous overflow still use complete baked bodies. Wounded survivors are never dismembered. Fragment motion is ballistic terrain contact, without collisions against living soldiers or other pieces.

Surviving artillery knockdowns use separate 25-pose `Knockdown` and `GetUp` sequences for both factions and both LODs. Recovery moves forward through a ground brace, half kneel and stand over 2.4 simulation seconds. The fall endpoint exactly matches the first recovery frame, and the last recovery frame exactly matches standing idle. The roster, wounded health and formation slot persist; soldiers cannot fire or fight while down or rising. Source poses are authored offline, with no per-soldier runtime skeleton.

## Flat knockdowns and limb contact

Infantry now selects one of four stable paired fall/recovery sequences per soldier. The front-down and back-down endpoints use a lateral quarter turn; knee and elbow IK chooses a bend point at ground height, and toes/fingers turn into the resting plane. Recovery varies the bracing hand, leading foot and initial sit-up/push-up posture. Each pair shares an identical down endpoint and finishes at standing idle. Common French/British poses retain 25 recovery samples and ten death variants; active roster libraries use 13 recovery samples and their grounded death sequence, with continuous GPU interpolation.

Ground placement measures the skinned body rather than the held musket. A downward-pointing weapon cannot lift an entire casualty; held muskets and command sidearms turn into the resting plane. Resting baked bodies align to the local terrain tangent. Physical artillery corpses use tighter collider contact offsets and relaxed shoulder/hip ranges, and freeze only when close to the ground and asleep or moving slowly.

`InfantryAssetBuild.BuildGroundedPreview` bakes the common library and builds the pose inspector. Inspect `Knockdown`, `GetUp` or `Death` with `--variant`, `--pose-phase` and `--ground-close`. `InfantryAssetBuild.FinishGroundedPlaytests` then updates command and active roster poses, checks all final mesh heights and recovery continuity with `GroundedInfantryVerification`, and rebuilds every active playtest. `BuildCombatEffects` runs both stages together. Ground-contact evidence belongs in `Artifacts/GroundContact`.

## Complete shell fragmentation and bullet wounds (development, 2026-10-01)

Strong lethal shell hits (impulse magnitude at least 7 m/s) now partition the posed French/British line infantry mesh into head, both arms, both legs and torso/equipment. Every original triangle belongs to exactly one fragment. Each connected cut opening receives a tissue cap. The intact corpse is suppressed immediately and its physics slot is released; subsequent casualties restore the complete source mesh. Real shell death records now preserve the supplied range-dependent blast impulse; the generic throw estimate is used only when no impulse was supplied. Less energetic artillery hits retain the existing single-limb severing probabilities. This path still uses the nearby, supported infantry ragdoll models; other factions, special roles, distant casualties and unavailable slots retain their baked casualty presentation.

Flying fragments emit ballistic blood droplets from their rotating wound points for up to 1.8 simulation seconds and add permanent landing stains. The existing 4,096-slot droplet budget applies; trails stop while paused and after settling. Fragments tumble and bounce on terrain, with orientation and mesh bounds center accounted for, and persist until restart. Fragment motion does not collide with soldiers or other fragments.

Nonfatal musket damage now emits a directional blood spurt for line volleys and targeted captain shots. A living wound produces droplet spatter, while a fatal bullet uses the existing death spray and one corpse pool. Pools activate after settling, spread and dry through the existing blood shader. Health, lethal damage and combat outcomes are unchanged.

## Living blast ragdolls

Strong nearby infantry knockdowns use at most twelve temporary ragdolls within 120 metres of the camera, sharing the existing 96-body global cap. Light hits, distant soldiers, overflow and command figures retain animated recovery. The complete uniform and equipment stay attached while the soldier is alive. Runtime skinning reuses the crowd mesh topology so a bounded 0.4-second vertex blend can bridge the settled ragdoll into the matching front/back get-up pose without a standing-pose snap. Recovery headings come from the settled torso. The temporary blend mesh is released at handoff.

Ragdoll motion follows the authoritative horizontal throw and retains the simulation's wounded health, formation identity, hit position and recovery deadline. Presentation availability never changes combat readiness. A lethal follow-up hit during tumbling or the pose blend converts the rig into a casualty and releases equipment only then. The crowd renderer suppresses a living soldier while his temporary rig is visible.

Shell shrapnel now emits 24 small fragments. Each fragment samples its travel budget once between 8 and 18 metres and its streak duration once between 0.003 and 0.007 seconds. Range clipping is applied before collision tests, including at coarse simulation timesteps; fragments stop at first contact or their bounded lifetime. Canister remains separate.

`SurvivorRagdollVerification.VerifyAndCapture` runs play-mode checks and offscreen recovery captures through the serialized Unity wrapper. It covers survival and death during both physical motion and recovery blending, cap/distance/light-hit fallbacks, unchanged gameplay positions and timing, fragment bounds/variation, and existing artillery recovery regressions. Captures are in `Artifacts/SurvivorRagdolls`. This verification does not publish a playtest.
