# Infantry prototype controls

The battle bindings follow the [official Empire: Total War manual, printed pages 8–9](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/10500/manuals/Manual_EN_LNX.pdf?t=1592491559). This is an approximate control implementation, not a recreation of the original interface or simulation.

WASD pans; holding either Shift key increases keyboard pan speed by 2.5×. Hold the middle mouse button and move the mouse to orient the camera in yaw and pitch. Pitch stays between 15° and 85° downward. Middle-button orientation cancels pending selection/frontage drags and suppresses new left/right mouse orders while held. These controls respect the runtime's `EnableInput` switch.

Q/E rotates, X/Z changes camera height, V/C changes pitch when no formation is selected; C orders a charge when formations are selected. End focuses the first selected battalion; Delete zooms into the first selected living battalion and follows its movement. Repeating Delete re-centers that following view. Manual camera pan, rotation or zoom releases tracking. Home focuses the first living French battalion as a temporary general proxy. Shift+Home restores the overview. Mouse wheel zoom keeps the terrain beneath the cursor fixed through the smooth transition. Camera distance spans 3–7,500 metres, with terrain clearance and slower close-range panning. Arrow keys also pan.

Press **M** to toggle the white military map at any zoom level or camera angle, with or without a selection. The chosen view stays on or off when zooming, panning, rotating or following a unit; toggling does not move the camera. Before the first manual toggle, at 180–500 metres above the focus terrain, the battlefield smoothly becomes an illustrated military atlas: printed meadow and field shading, paired road lines, dotted hedgerows/farm fences with real gate openings, woodland outlines and tree symbols, contour lines and hill hatching, the current camera angle, blue French and red British formation bars, sparse regiment labels, and cannon ticks. Bars preserve frontage and facing, square formations are hollow, routing units fade, and selected units gain a gold border. Clicking a bar still selects the actual unit. In automatic mode, zooming back in restores the realistic terrain and soldiers. Hold Space in this view to show broad translucent blue movement arrows, red attack arrows with brackets around their targets, and outlined destination formations; battery attack paths are combined when cannons share the same order. Right-drag previews use the same map symbols.

National flags appear directly above living formations at middle distances and fade away as atlas symbols appear. Crowded flags are omitted, with selected units taking priority; flags never stack or move away from their regiments. Hover a flag to see its unit name and strength. Selected formations retain their flags up close, show brass ground outlines, and show rings beneath individual soldiers at close range.

Click a French formation, banner, or bottom unit card to select it. Drag left mouse to select several. Ctrl-click toggles additive selection; Shift is an additional additive shortcut. Double-click a card focuses its unit. Ctrl+M selects available French infantry and cannons. Ctrl+1–9 assigns groups and 1–9 recalls them.

Selected units show a blue move arrow over terrain and a red attack crosshair over enemy formations. Dragging right mouse to set frontage previews movement. Selected artillery shows the attack crosshair over terrain because its ground order is bombardment. HUD controls, camera orientation and empty selection use the normal cursor. Selected unit and battery orders live in compact centered panels above the roster; hover the regiment name for its equipment and formation details.

Battles always use clear dawn lighting (07:00). Lighting and weather controls are removed from the battle screen.

Right-click moves to a destination using the selected infantry’s current walk/run setting while preserving relative group positions and facing the direction of travel. Soldiers keep their assigned rank and file while marching. R toggles run/walk for selected infantry, including the next move; double-right-click enables running. Right-drag draws a frontage: the segment points along the formation's rightward tangent, with facing perpendicular to it. Selected battalions divide the frontage with gaps in their existing physical order; width determines files and ranks. Blue ground slots and arrows preview the deployment. Escape cancels a drag. Hold Shift while right-clicking to append move waypoints, then Shift-right-click an enemy to queue an attack after those moves. Shift-right-drag queues a frontage/facing change; Shift+C queues a charge. Queued orders start in sequence once the preceding move and turn finish; attacks continue until the target is eliminated. Normal right-click replaces the queue, and H/Backspace clears it. Orders can be queued while paused. Hold Space in either terrain or white map mode to preview the entire queue: connected blue movement paths and destination formations, followed by red attack lines and target formations. Idle units are not highlighted by the order overlay. P toggles pause. The top bar has separate Pause, 0.25×, 1×, 2× and 4× buttons; selecting a speed resumes playback. Ctrl+T cycles these speeds. Camera movement remains at normal speed during slow motion and pause. CINEMA toggles filmic grading, restrained bloom and vignette; FOCUS toggles close-up background depth of field. Focus follows the camera's formation/terrain target, fades out when pulling back, and leaves the foreground sharp. The atlas view fades out the cinematic grade.

L selects preset line, K preset column, J skirmish order, and N hollow square. **[** decreases ranks and **]** increases ranks in the current formation; the selected-unit panel shows the current depth. Squares support five or six ranks for a 250-man regiment, with a maximum depth based on surviving strength that preserves their hollow center. Rectangular blocks use the nearest available file count when an exact depth is impossible. F toggles melee mode; the FIRE button toggles fire at will. Backspace or H halts, and R toggles run/walk. Withdrawal is available through the WITHDRAW button and has no hotkey. These formation shortcuts are prototype additions. Dragging custom frontage supports deep blocks with at least four files and at least two ranks; rank hotkeys can create a single firing rank.

For infantry, enemy right-click assigns a ranged attack target and enables fire. Infantry hold their current position and turn toward that target by default; they fire once it is within range and their firing arc. Press G to follow targets and close to musket standoff distance. Disabling pursuit stops an existing chase. When an assigned enemy routes or is destroyed, ranged infantry and cannons switch to their nearest surviving, non-routing enemy. Movement and halt orders replace the infantry attack order. Selection cards use military map symbols, strength counts and status labels. Hover order buttons and fire modes for their benefits and tradeoffs. Shared woodland zones slow movement and obstruct long firing lines; individual tree trunks remain decorative. Cavalry are selectable squadrons of 60 riders. Enemy right-click orders their charge; **C** charges the current or nearest enemy for selected infantry or cavalry. **F** or the MELEE button toggles melee mode: the sword cursor indicates that enemy right-click will charge instead of firing. **Alt + enemy right-click** orders an infantry bayonet charge. Cavalry use line or column, accelerate into contact, lose momentum on impact, then pull through or turn away to reform before another charge. Routing enemies can be pursued for twenty simulation seconds after victory.

Substantial facing changes use individual turns and nearby formation slots. An about-face keeps complete ranks in place; incomplete ranks make small adjustments. Quarter-turns, line/column changes and frontage changes redistribute soldiers without teleporting them. Small course corrections still wheel. Marching waits for a substantial turn to settle and slows when soldiers fall behind; orderly withdrawal preserves the unit’s facing.

### Skirmish and square

Skirmish order spreads soldiers to 2.6-metre spacing. Settled skirmishers aim, fire and reload kneeling with independent shot and reload timing, and stand up to move. Their muskets avoid lanes occupied by their own men. Open order reduces the probability of musket and artillery hits; cavalry contact inflicts increased damage.

Squares use four outward-facing walls around a hollow center. Their default depth is three ranks for a full regiment. The outer rank kneels and continuously presents aimed muskets and fixed bayonets; the next rank fires independently into its outward sector. Inner ranks provide reserves. Squares fire on all sides without turning the entire formation toward one enemy. Formed, stationary squares resist cavalry contact and deal increased bayonet damage to cavalry; moving or unsettled squares lose that protection. Square protection comes from the actual contacted wall, its facing and nearby supporting soldiers. Flank/rear attacks, gaps, loose order and unfinished formation changes are more vulnerable.

### Infantry firing drill

The fire-mode button shows the current mode, or MIXED when selected regiments differ. The dropdown highlights the active mode; hover any option for its benefits, tradeoffs and role default. **B** cycles through the same three modes. Line infantry and guards default to **platoon fire**, light infantry and riflemen to **fire at will**, and militia to **volley fire**.

**Fire at will** gives the highest sustained firing rate and normal casualty morale loss. **Platoon fire** balances rate and morale pressure: casualties cause 1.5× normal morale loss, and groups of at most 16 files sweep each rank before advancing through the next, up to four ranks deep. Rank firing is part of the platoon drill, not a separate mode. **Volley fire** delivers the strongest morale shock—2.5× morale loss per casualty—with the lowest sustained firing rate and a longer wait to reload and coordinate the full frontage. These modes do not change bullet damage or accuracy. Squares and skirmish formations always use independent fire. Fired soldiers kneel to reload; lower ranks stay down while those behind them fire. Moving, changing formation, charging, routing, or holding fire stops the drill. Soldiers still moving into place sit out the discharge; their ready platoon mates keep firing. Use the **FIRE** button to toggle fire at will; **F** switches melee mode.

### Cannon batteries

Artillery is grouped into units of four cannons, each starting with 250 rounds. Click a French cannon to select its entire unit. Left-drag selects whole artillery units and infantry together; Ctrl/Shift-click adds or removes an entire artillery unit. Numbered control groups preserve mixed infantry and cannon selections. Selected cannons have ground outlines and dashed lines to their ordered targets.

Right-click an enemy formation to track and fire at it, or right-click terrain to bombard that position. Guns wait when an ordered target is outside the selected ammunition's range. The cannon panel shows selection strength, ammunition, readiness, held guns and range status. The cannon panel’s fire/hold button toggles firing. Backspace or H clears orders and holds fire, and AUTO TARGET restores automatic target selection. Cannons remain stationary.

Choose ammunition in the cannon panel; the choice applies to all selected guns. Changing a loaded gun's ammunition starts a reload. Each discharge uses one ammunition round, including a canister discharge.

- **ROUND:** roundshot, a 7.5% chance of one energy-losing ground skip and penetration through formations; 900 m range.
- **CANISTER:** twelve short-lived pellets in a cone; 220 m range.
- **SHELL:** explosive blast with outward/upward casualty throws; 900 m range.
- **CARCASS:** smaller initial blast followed by 90 seconds of burning ground across an 18 m radius; 900 m range.
- **LIME:** a 60-second dust cloud that deals light damage and reduces accuracy for the rest of the battle; 900 m range.
- **POISON GAS:** a yellow cloud that hangs low for 45 seconds over a 20 m radius; up to 6 damage immediately and every two seconds; surviving units move slower for the rest of the battle. Affects both armies. Unlocks at Artillery munitions rank 5; 900 m range.

Burning ground and lime clouds affect troops from either army. These are gameplay approximations. Battery guns distribute their aim across the central mass of living troops and reverse coverage on successive salvos. Accuracy upgrades tighten artillery scatter but cannot remove it; the minimum scatter grows with range. Musket and artillery spread grows with range; distant cannon trails and a subset of musket trails help show firing direction. Musket muzzle smoke lasts 7–10 seconds, with pooled powder clouds lingering 80–130 seconds.

Selected infantry displays a fan starting at the two outer edges of its front rank, with a solid nominal-range boundary and dashed maximum-range boundary. The fan widens outward and joins rounded shoulders across the full frontage; squares display their full circular firing range. Selected guns display their ammunition range, minimum targeting distance, and current facing. These are range limits; obstacles and friendly units can still prevent fire.

### Camera cannon in the playtest

With no infantry or cannons selected, right-click terrain to fire a roundshot from the camera at the mouse aim point. Left-click empty terrain to clear the selection first. The ball uses the shared artillery pool, gravity, impact effects, penetration and ground skips, without using a gun's ammunition. Right-click continues to issue movement or attack orders when units are selected. Space remains the held order-overlay key.

### Contact combat and mounted animation

Only soldiers with a reachable opponent strike. Infantry use bayonet thrusts, parries, butt strikes, recoil and stagger poses; rear ranks cannot thrust through closer allies. Outer square walls retain kneeling bayonets. Mounted squadrons use walk, trot, gallop, charge, left/right sabre attacks, braking, turning and stumbling. Fallen mounts and riders use separate casualty poses. These are procedural prototype animations based on the shared source infantry and horse, with GPU pose blending at both detail levels.

Repeatable development encounters use `--melee-case infantry|frontal|flank|skirmish|square|forming|routing`; these replace the ordinary deployment and suppress AI and shooting. Combine with the usual benchmark and screenshot flags for comparison.

Regimental standards now carry larger silk colours on raised staffs, with national flags, varied regimental fields, gold borders and matching pennants. Their cloth uses fixed-step fabric constraints, gravity, shared wind gusts and a marching response; pausing freezes the cloth. Settlement objective banners use the same cloth simulation.

All artillery ammunition can ricochet once on a sufficiently fast, non-vertical terrain impact (7.5% chance). Explosive fillings discharge at the next impact after a skip. Lime wounds exposed troops and gun crews immediately and every three seconds (up to 6 health per pulse, falling with distance). It reduces accuracy for the remainder of the battle and interrupts gun service while exposed. Leaving the cloud stops damage; staying near the center can be lethal.
